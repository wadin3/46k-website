**Machine-operational brand specification**

This document is written for AI systems producing text, design
direction, image prompts, web/UI concepts, reports, presentations,
social content, video concepts, player-facing materials, or other 46K
assets.

It is not marketing copy. Treat it as a constraint system.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# 2. EVIDENCE, CLAIMS, AND DEGREE OF CONFIDENCE

This section is mandatory for all factual/scientific content.

## 2.1 Evidence ladder

Classify substantive claims internally before writing:

### LEVEL A --- WE KNOW

Use for established general psychological/cognitive knowledge with
strong evidence.

Language may be clear and authoritative.

### LEVEL B --- EVIDENCE SUGGESTS

Use for promising but incomplete football-specific evidence,
correlations, replications with limitations, or findings that do not
justify certainty at the individual level.

Language may be confident but must preserve uncertainty.

### LEVEL C --- WE THINK

Use for 46K hypotheses, interpretations, extrapolations, development
ideas, recommendations, or "expert guesses".

These may be bold. They must be identifiable as interpretation rather
than established fact.

## 2.2 Never do the following

Do not: - claim a cognitive test measures a player's exact "true
football intelligence" - claim a test can determine who will become a
professional player - turn correlation into causation - turn group-level
findings into certain individual predictions - present theoretical
derivation as empirical evidence - present promising evidence as settled
science - use unsupported superlatives such as "best", "most accurate",
or "better than anyone" - imply that 46K replaces coaches, scouts, match
observation, or football expertise

Preferred conceptual framing: **46K adds information that football is
currently less good at seeing.**

## 2.3 Brand confidence rule

Do not make front-facing copy timid merely to signal scientific caution.

The method should withstand scrutiny.  
The brand should still create curiosity, desire, and competitive
relevance.

Correct pattern: 1. state what is supported 2. distinguish uncertainty
where necessary 3. make a useful 46K interpretation 4. connect it back
to football

# 3. VERBAL IDENTITY

## 3.1 Voice

46K sounds intelligent **without trying to sound intelligent**.

Default language characteristics: - concise - clear - informed -
confident - slightly dry - contemporary - football-aware - low on
jargon - willing to make a point

Core rule: **Complex science. Simple language.**

Do not simplify the science itself. Simplify the explanation.

## 3.2 Writing instructions

Do: - know the science; do not show off vocabulary - make a point rather
than merely summarizing information - translate implications into
football - challenge methods, assumptions, and blind spots - distinguish
evidence from 46K interpretation - use short sentences when impact is
useful - allow dry, intelligent self-awareness

Do not: - sound like an academic abstract unless the requested format
requires it - sound like management consulting - sound like generic
startup marketing - sound like a motivational speaker - overuse "neuro",
"brain", "elite", "performance", or "innovation" - use trend slang to
sound young - manufacture hype

Avoid unless specifically justified: - revolutionary - groundbreaking -
cutting-edge - next-generation - game-changing - disruptive

## 3.3 Humor

Allowed: - dry - intelligent - sparse - self-aware - slightly irreverent

Not allowed: - clowning - meme-factory behavior - childish humor -
trying to sound like a teenager - humiliating
players/coaches/researchers - attacking individuals rather than ideas

## 3.4 Staccato copy

Staccato is a **display tool**, not the default speaking style.

Good: **UNDERSTAND. ASSESS. DEVELOP.**

Possible when context supports it: **SEE IT. BEFORE THEY DO.**

Do not make every headline a three-word sequence. Do not escalate into
macho slogans such as "DOMINATE. DESTROY. WIN."

# 4. THE THREE GEARS

46K has **one identity** with three modes.

They are not sub-brands.

The central rule is:

**THE GEAR FOLLOWS THE CONTENT --- NOT THE CHANNEL.**

A website, Instagram account, report, presentation, or video series may
use more than one gear.

## 4.1 EDITORIAL

**Function:** ideas, insights, perspectives

Use for: - research interpretation - essays - 2046 narrative - larger
questions - talent-development systems - long-form storytelling -
reflective brand communication

Feeling: - calm - intelligent - spacious - human - lightly romantic
about football - credible without academic stiffness

Visual tendency: - Paper-dominant - light/faded grayscale photography -
restrained Gold - serif editorial headlines - subtle print/paper
materiality - short gold line as a recurring marker

Maximum intensity: Do not increase nostalgia or football romanticism
beyond a restrained editorial-magazine level. Avoid retro pastiche.

## 4.2 CORE

**Function:** tests, data, development

Use for: - assessment - player profiles - test product - data -
feedback - recommendations - development plans - research translated
into product logic - result interfaces

Feeling: - precise - contemporary - useful - intelligent - calm but not
sterile

Visual tendency: - balanced Paper / Pitch / Graphite - simple
test-inspired forms - dots, sequences, shapes, stimuli - radar/spider
charts where appropriate - player portraits with information overlays -
coaching, feedback, training cones/bibs - clear connection from
measurement to development

Important: 46K cognitive tests are intended primarily for **mobile**,
not desktop computer testing. If a laptop/computer appears in Core
photography, video review/analysis is usually more appropriate than
showing cognitive testing.

Avoid outward-facing technical test labels such as "N-back" unless the
content specifically requires them.

## 4.3 PERFORMANCE

**Function:** training, match, results

Use for: - player-facing communication - training - competition - match
situations - perception - fast decisions - performance application -
high-energy social/video moments

Feeling: - faster - darker - more physical - competitive - still
intelligent - never macho

Visual tendency: - Graphite/Pitch-dominant - closer framing - movement -
training at least as important as match imagery - visual fields -
arcs/radar forms - angles - arrows - time values / fractions of
seconds - selective focus - blurred opponents or environment - visual
cues that suggest information retained in working memory - cones and
training details to signal development

Avoid: - gaming HUD overload - superhero treatment - excessive goal
celebrations - chest-beating masculinity - generic "beast mode"
aesthetics - coordinate clutter - football-broadcast graphics copied
literally

# 5. COLOR SYSTEM

These values are locked.

## 5.1 Primary tokens

- **46K Paper:** `#F5F2E9`
- **46K Graphite:** `#383D3E`
- **46K Pitch:** `#285C43`
- **46K Gold:** `#C29D45`

Core rule: **Paper, Graphite and Pitch build the world. Gold points to
what matters.**

## 5.2 Color behavior

### Paper

Use as: - primary light background - editorial field - report/article
surface - calm web sections

It should feel like warm paper, not beige lifestyle branding and not
sterile white.

Allowed treatment: - subtle grain - subtle fibre - subtle print texture

Do not: - age it into vintage parchment - make it dirty/grungy - turn it
yellow/brown

### Graphite

Use as: - primary text - dark surface - primary dark alternative to
black

Prefer Graphite over pure black in brand-controlled design.

### Pitch

Use as: - football-linked field color - Core/Performance surface -
section background - structured accent

It must read clearly as green next to Graphite.

Do not shift toward: - lime - neon - gaming green - sports-drink green -
generic sustainability/eco green

### Gold

Use as: - marker - selected letter K in primary logo - key datapoint -
short line - important numeral - selected annotation - 2046/ambition
emphasis - restrained display accent

Do not use Gold as: - ordinary body text - default large background -
broad decorative wash - generic luxury signal

Rule: **Gold is a marker, not wallpaper.**

## 5.3 Brushed Gold treatment

Allowed: - subtle abrasion - restrained lighter highlights - restrained
darker scratches - print/metal/trophy handling feel - physical
imperfection

The overall perceived color must remain near `#C29D45`.

Do not: - make it cardboard - make it muddy ochre - use shiny metallic
gradients - use chrome - use casino/Rolex/champagne-packaging effects

## 5.4 Functional data colors

The primary brand palette is not required to encode every data series.

If additional functional colors are necessary: - create a subordinate
functional palette - keep saturation controlled - maintain sufficient
contrast - do not let secondary colors become brand colors - never rely
on color alone to communicate meaning - also use labels, shape,
position, line style, or pattern

# 6. TYPOGRAPHY SYSTEM

The conceptual typography roles are locked. Exact production font
families are **not yet locked**.

Do not invent a "final official font" unless the user explicitly
approves one.

## 6.1 CORE typography

Required characteristics: - thin/light weight - uppercase for key
display typography - normal letter proportions - generous
tracking/spacing where appropriate - precise - modern - elegant - not
futuristic

CRITICAL: **CORE IS THIN/LIGHT, NOT NARROW/CONDENSED.**

Do not interpret "thin" as "compressed".

## 6.2 PERFORMANCE typography

Required characteristics: - bold/black - uppercase - may be somewhat
compact - may be italic/slanted - visually moving - forceful without
becoming jersey typography

Condensed typography may be used here if it serves the composition, but
it is not mandatory.

Avoid: - hockey jersey lettering - college athletics - gym poster
typography - YouTube-thumbnail aggression - extreme sports cliché

## 6.3 EDITORIAL typography

Required characteristics: - sober serif - normal capitalization: initial
capital + lowercase in normal headings - moderate weight - generous
space - editorial / magazine / research feel

Avoid: - luxury perfume serif - fashion-house excess - faux newspaper
nostalgia - decorative vintage serif

## 6.4 Functional typography

Use a modern, neutral, highly readable sans-serif for: - body text -
navigation - UI - labels - charts - tables - test interfaces - metadata

Core summary: **Core is light and precise. Performance is heavy and
moving. Editorial is quiet and considered.**

# 8. GRAPHIC LANGUAGE

46K graphic language should make the viewer feel that football
information is being observed, decoded, measured, retained, or
translated into development.

Primary principle: **Visualize information --- not just football.**

Secondary principle: **Visualize thinking through football.**

Development principle: **Measurement should lead toward development.**

## 8.1 Information graphics

Allowed recurring elements: - datapoints - dots - lines - scales -
profiles - measurements - annotations - restrained grids - radar/spider
profiles - bars - development-over-time graphics

Graphics must appear purposeful, not like decorative "tech".

## 8.2 Test language

Allowed: - simple stimuli - dots - shapes - sequences - choices - visual
changes - abstract elements inspired by actual 46K tests

When final 46K tests exist: prefer borrowing visual elements from the
real test system.

Do not bind the entire identity to one current test battery.

Do not use glowing brains or neural-network imagery.

## 8.3 Perception language

Allowed: - field-of-view arcs - attention markers - angles - selective
highlighting - arrows when they communicate information flow or
options - time values / fractions of seconds - selective focus - a
previously observed player kept sharp while other elements blur - simple
pitch zones with points/lines

Important: A pitch graphic should not automatically look like tactical
analysis.

46K is primarily interested in: - perception - information - cognition -
decisions - development

not generic formation/tactics diagrams.

## 8.4 Materiality

Allowed: - subtle Paper grain - faded grayscale - brushed/worn Gold -
restrained print texture - controlled physical imperfection

Recurring micro-motif: **short Gold line**

Use it as a subtle marker, not a mandatory decoration on every asset.

# 9. PHOTOGRAPHY AND IMAGERY

## 9.1 Subject age

Do not default to children.

Even when communicating to 15-year-old players, imagery should generally
feature: - older teenagers - young adults - adult players

The viewer may aspire toward the player shown.

## 9.2 Representation

Use gender, ethnicity, football level, and environment naturally.

Include: - women and men - different ethnic backgrounds - academy/elite
environments - ordinary clubs - grassroots / lower divisions -
individual training - coaching - analysis

Do not make diversity feel like visible checkbox casting.

## 9.3 General photography rule

**46K does not merely photograph football stars. 46K photographs
football players thinking, learning, perceiving, deciding, and
developing.**

Football context should remain perceptible even in Core.

## 9.4 Editorial imagery

Use: - light/faded grayscale - low-to-moderate contrast - dreamlike or
reflective looks - stadium and local football ground - quiet football
details - restrained football romanticism

Do not: - make grayscale too dark/black-heavy against Paper - use
dramatic noir contrast - become nostalgic/retro

## 9.5 Core imagery

Use: - calmer light - portraits - concentration - mobile assessment -
video review - coach giving instructions - feedback conversations -
cones - training bibs - individual development - subtle data overlays

Development must be visible often enough that Core does not read as
"testing company".

## 9.6 Performance imagery

Use: - motion - darker treatment - training and match - closer framing -
physical effort - visual focus - perception overlays - blurred
opposition/environment - timing and option cues

Training imagery is at least as important as match imagery.

Avoid: - goal-celebration cliché - superstar worship - exaggerated
aggression - smoke, lightning, fire, neon energy - generic "warrior"
imagery

# 10. LAYOUT, DATA, AND UI

## 10.1 Layout

Use: - strong hierarchy - generous negative space - disciplined grid -
responsive behavior - asymmetry when controlled - large clear focal
points

Do not: - fill empty space for its own sake - create dense dashboard
walls - use decorative cards everywhere - over-segment content

## 10.2 UI

UI should feel: - understated - functional - intelligent - calm -
contemporary

It must not feel like: - generic SaaS dashboard - crypto product -
gaming interface - medical software - corporate HR platform

## 10.3 Data

Primary rule: **Complex information. Clear presentation.**

Suitable: - radar/spider chart - bars - trend/development over time -
simple pitch/information visualization - clear percentile/profile views
where scientifically justified

Do not: - use a rainbow of colors by default - make data decorative at
the expense of comprehension - imply false precision - display
pseudo-scientific numbers merely to look advanced

## 10.4 Product flow

Player-facing product experiences should tend toward:

**RESULT → INSIGHT → RECOMMENDATION → TRAINING → FOLLOW-UP**

Do not let assessment terminate at a score.

Development is a core product promise.

# 12. CONTENT ANATOMY

Useful default logic:

**HOOK → INSIGHT → EVIDENCE → 46K TAKE → APPLICATION**

Not every asset requires all five stages.

## 12.1 Hook

Use: - strong question - observation - contradiction - surprising fact -
clear thesis

Do not use: - clickbait disconnected from the evidence - fear-based
selling - manufactured controversy

## 12.2 Insight

Explain what is actually interesting.

## 12.3 Evidence

State what the evidence supports at the appropriate WE KNOW / EVIDENCE
SUGGESTS level.

## 12.4 46K Take

46K should be willing to interpret, hypothesize, or propose.

Label the epistemic status correctly.

## 12.5 Application

Answer: **So what does this mean for football?**

Possible targets: - player - coach - scout - academy - club - parent -
Swedish football system

# 13. AUDIENCE CALIBRATION

The brand personality does not change. Depth, examples, and vocabulary
do.

## 13.1 Player

Primary question: **Can this tell me something about myself and help me
get better?**

Emphasize: - self-knowledge - competitive information advantage -
development - concrete football situations - curiosity - "I want to know
how good I am at this"

Use less technical language.

Do not talk down to the player.

## 13.2 Coach

Primary question: **Can this help me understand and develop the player
better?**

Emphasize: - observable football situations - development - individual
differences - actionable interpretation

## 13.3 Scout / academy / club

Primary question: **Can this give us useful information that traditional
evaluation may miss?**

Emphasize: - complementary information - player profiles -
selection/development decisions - information advantage - method and
evidence

Do not promise deterministic talent prediction.

## 13.4 Parent

Primary question: **Is this credible, understandable, and useful for my
child's development?**

Emphasize: - credibility - development - understanding strengths -
realistic limits

Never imply guaranteed elite progression.

## 13.5 Researcher / psychologist

Primary question: **Does the reasoning and methodology hold up?**

Emphasize: - sources - constructs - validity - limitations - distinction
between evidence and hypothesis - methodological transparency

Do not inflate certainty.

Core rule: **Simplify the language, not the idea.**

# 17. DESIGN DECISION TREE

Before producing a visual asset, answer internally:

### Step 1 --- What is the content doing?

If mainly: - ideas / research / perspective / 2046 → **EDITORIAL** -
assessment / data / profile / recommendation / development system →
**CORE** - training / match / performance / player motivation /
application → **PERFORMANCE**

If mixed: choose one dominant gear and borrow limited elements from
another.

Do not create an equal three-way visual mixture unless the asset is
specifically demonstrating the brand system.

### Step 2 --- What should the viewer feel?

Editorial: "I want to think about this."

Core: "I understand something I could not see before."

Performance: "This information could help me perform/develop."

### Step 3 --- What is the information hierarchy?

Define: 1. one primary idea 2. one secondary explanation 3. supporting
evidence/data 4. optional action

Do not give all information equal visual weight.

### Step 4 --- Is Gold pointing to something meaningful?

If no: remove it.

### Step 5 --- Is development visible where relevant?

If the asset concerns assessment/player profiling: show or communicate
the path toward development.

### Evidence discipline

Internally classify substantive claims as: - **WE KNOW** = established
knowledge - **EVIDENCE SUGGESTS** = promising/incomplete evidence - **WE
THINK** = 46K interpretation/hypothesis/recommendation

Never turn correlation into causation, group findings into certain
individual predictions, or promising evidence into settled science.
Never claim a test determines a player's true football intelligence or
future professional success. 46K complements coaches, scouts,
observation, and match data; it does not replace them.

### Voice

Use concise, clear, informed language. **Complex science. Simple
language.** Make a point; do not merely summarize. Challenge ideas and
methods, not people. Humor may be dry, sparse, and self-aware. Avoid
startup hype, gratuitous "neuro" language, motivational-speaker tone,
fake youth slang, and academic stiffness. Staccato is emphasis, not
normal speech.

Choose the gear from the content, not the channel.

**EDITORIAL = ideas, insights, perspectives** Use for research, essays,
2046, larger questions, storytelling. Visual: Paper-dominant, faded
light grayscale, restrained Gold, sober serif, subtle print materiality.

**CORE = tests, data, development** Use for assessment, player profiles,
data, product, feedback, recommendations, development. Visual:
Paper/Pitch/Graphite balance, simple test-inspired forms, radar/profile
data, portraits/information overlays, coaching/development cues.
Typography: thin/light uppercase with normal proportions. **Never
interpret Core as condensed/narrow.**

**PERFORMANCE = training, match, results** Use for player-facing
performance, training, competition, perception, decisions. Visual:
darker Graphite/Pitch, movement, selective blur, perception arcs,
angles, restrained arrows, fractions of seconds, training details.
Typography: bold/black uppercase, optionally slanted/italic. Never
become macho, gaming-like, or superhero-like.
