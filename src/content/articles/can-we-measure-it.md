If we want to measure something, we first need to say what that something is.

That sounds straightforward. In football, it rarely is.

## What exactly would a test capture?

A cognitive task may be designed to examine attention, memory, inhibition or flexibility. But performing well on a task and performing well in a match are not interchangeable outcomes.

A score only becomes useful when we know what the task measures reliably, how it relates to the intended construct and what its limitations are.

## Three questions for every measure

1. **Construct:** Which capability are we claiming to examine?
2. **Evidence:** Does the task provide reliable and valid information for that purpose?
3. **Football relevance:** Does the information add anything useful alongside observation and match context?

The third question is particularly important for 46K. A technically consistent test is not automatically useful for football.

> Measurement is the beginning of an explanation — not its replacement.

## The evidence ladder

**WE KNOW:** Psychological measurement requires attention to reliability, validity and the interpretation of individual scores.

**EVIDENCE SUGGESTS:** Certain cognitive capabilities may relate to aspects of sport performance, but findings should be evaluated study by study, including their methods and limitations.

**WE THINK:** A carefully validated set of measures could eventually provide useful complementary information for players, coaches and clubs.

We are not claiming that 46K currently has such a validated system. Developing and evaluating it is part of the work.

## What would success look like?

Not a single number that predicts a career. A more useful result would be a defensible profile that sparks better questions and informs carefully considered development — if research supports that use.
