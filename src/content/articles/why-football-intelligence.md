  - kind: text
    heading: The visible action is not the whole event
    body: >-
      **We tend to start with what we can see.** A pass through a defensive line. A movement that
      opens space. A decision to wait when everyone else wants to go forward. These are useful
      observations. But the action itself does not explain everything about how the player
      arrived at it.


      In our imagined situation, the same pass might have several explanations. The player might
      have recognised a familiar pattern, noticed a new movement or followed a coaching
      instruction. We need to ask what came before the action, not simply judge how elegantly it
      ended.


      **46K's interpretation:** A more useful discussion begins by separating observation from
      assumption. That distinction gives the coach more questions to ask. It does not give us
      permission to read a player's mind from a video clip.

  - kind: text
    heading: One word, several meanings
    body: >-
      Football intelligence is a useful football expression. It can also become a container for
      almost everything we admire in a player. In one conversation it means finding space. In
      another it means understanding a teammate, choosing a risk or changing a decision.


      [Adele Diamond's 2013 review](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-113011-143750)
      describes inhibition, working memory and cognitive flexibility as core executive
      functions. These are psychological concepts we can work with. They do not, by themselves,
      define or validate football intelligence.


      For 46K, *translation therefore requires care*. We can ask what kinds of information a
      situation demands. We cannot equate a cognitive term with everything happening in the
      game. Football's own details have to remain in the picture. Otherwise a more technical
      vocabulary may simply disguise an equally broad assumption.
  - kind: quote
    text: A label is not yet a definition. And a definition is not yet a measurement.
    attribution: 46K / Extract from this article
  - kind: text
    heading: Start with a better observation
    body: >-
      Imagine the coach and player reviewing a sequence after training. Instead of beginning
      with right or wrong, the coach asks which option first became visible. What changed when
      the opponent moved? Which option did not feel available?


      The answers are not an exact recording of the player's mental processes. They are part of
      a conversation. The coach can compare them with the footage, the team's task and other
      situations over time. Uncertainty in an answer is worth preserving too.


      Our editorial starting point is curiosity before labels. Describe a player as often
      looking towards a particular area rather than possessing a fixed quantity of football
      intelligence. The first statement can be followed up. The second risks sounding more final
      than the evidence allows. A good question should leave room for a different answer.
  - kind: model
    heading: From action to question
    steps:
      - title: Observe
        text: What actually happens in the selected sequence?
      - title: Separate
        text: What do we observe, and what do we assume?
      - title: Ask
        text: How does the player describe the situation?
      - title: Return
        text: What do we want to look for next time?
    caption: >-

  - kind: text
    heading: When the same decision means different things
    body: >-
      A safe pass can be a sensible choice in one situation and a missed opportunity in another.
      That depends on the task, teammates' movements and the position the team is trying to
      create. Remove the context and the assessment becomes thinner, however confident its
      wording.


      In our example, we need to know more than whether the pass arrived. What was the team
      trying to do? Which options were available to the player, rather than only to the camera?
      What happened immediately beforehand?


      It is tempting to search for one sign that separates the intelligent player from everyone
      else. Our working idea moves in another direction. We want complementary perspectives to
      meet. Observation, conversation and possible measurements should illuminate the same
      player without pretending to be the same kind of information.

    label: EVIDENCE NOTE
  - kind: text
    heading: A question for the next training session
    body: >-
      Choose one situation you want to understand better. Describe it concretely enough for
      coach and player to know which moment you mean. Keep the distinction between the visible
      sequence and your interpretation.


      The next step need not be a larger system. It might be returning to the question a week
      later: are we seeing something different, or have we become better at describing what we
      already saw? Either answer can be useful in honest development work.


      Football intelligence does not become less interesting when we acknowledge how difficult
      the concept is. We can leave the quick label behind and investigate what we actually want
      to understand. For 46K, that is a reasonable beginning: better language, a clearer
      question and a player who is still more than our explanation.

The ball is travelling towards the midfielder. There is time for one touch, perhaps two. A defender approaches from behind. On the left, a teammate has already started running. From the touchline, the choice becomes obvious only when the pass is played. For the player, it has to become clear earlier.

This scene is fictional, but its question is worth staying with: what do we mean when we say someone reads the game? We are trying out a language for the question, not offering a complete explanation of a player's performance.
