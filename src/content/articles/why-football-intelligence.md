Football is full of moments that look simple only after they have happened.

A pass arrives. A defender moves. A space opens, and closes. One player reacts to what is in front of them. Another seems to have anticipated it.

What makes the difference?

## More than a good decision

When people describe a player as *intelligent*, they may mean several different things: awareness, positioning, anticipation, creativity, decision-making or tactical understanding.

Those qualities overlap in everyday football language. They are not necessarily the same psychological ability.

That distinction matters. Before we can ask how to measure football intelligence, we need to be clearer about what we mean by it.

> A label is not yet a definition. And a definition is not yet a measurement.

## From observation to understanding

Coaches and scouts observe football performance in context. Their expertise matters. But an observation of a successful action does not always tell us which capabilities produced it.

46K is exploring whether cognitive science can offer useful additional perspectives on that question.

That does **not** mean a simple test can tell us who is a better footballer. It means testing a more modest possibility: could well-chosen cognitive measures, alongside football observation, deepen our understanding of how players process information?

## Our starting position

**WE THINK:** A useful model of football intelligence should help us distinguish the abilities we want to understand, identify what can responsibly be measured and explain how any insight might inform development.

This is an ambition for research and development, not a validated assessment system.

The task ahead is to move from an interesting question to evidence that can withstand scrutiny.
